📋 POCUS Protocol (Bedside, 5 min)

  • Cardiac (PLAX/PSAX/A4C): LV function, RV dilation, pericardial effusion, IVC diameter + collapsibility
  • IVC: <2cm + >50% collapse = volume responsive. >2cm fixed = high CVP, do NOT push fluids
  • Lungs: B-lines ≥3 bilateral = cardiogenic or ARDS. A-lines = no pulmonary edema
  • Abdomen: Free fluid = hemorrhagic shock source?
  • DVT views: Bilateral femoral/popliteal compression if PE suspected

🌡️ Clinical Stratification — Who Gets Vasopressors Now?

IMMEDIATE — Start Now

  • MAP <65 after 1-2L crystalloid (or 500ml if cardiogenic concern)
  • Septic shock (SSC 2021: start NE early — don't delay for fluid)
  • Anaphylaxis with hemodynamic collapse → IM Epi FIRST
  • Cardiac arrest / PEA → IV Epi 1mg q3-5min
  • MAP <50 at any point regardless of fluid status
  • Mottling score ≥3 or oliguria <0.3ml/kg/hr >1hr

HIGH ALERT — Prepare & Monitor

  • SBP 80-90 despite 1L fluid given
  • qSOFA ≥2 (RR>22, AMS, SBP<100)
  • Lactate 2-4 mmol/L (intermediate risk)
  • Declining urine output after 2 boluses
  • Trending up NE dose >0.1 mcg/kg/min
  • New requirement for O2 in previously room-air patient

WATCHFUL WAITING

  • SBP 90-100 responding to fluid bolus
  • Warm extremities, CRT <2s, good mentation
  • qSOFA 0-1, no evidence of end-organ dysfunction
  • Lactate <2 mmol/L, trending down after fluids
  • HR 90-110 with volume response
  • Reassess every 30 minutes

Recognition of Haemodynamic Deterioration

Early warning signs before circulatory collapse — identify the trajectory, not just the threshold. A trend is more dangerous than a number.

⚠️
Key principle: You're looking for the TREND, not a single value. A patient with BP 95/60 that was 110/70 thirty minutes ago is in more danger than one with chronic BP 90/55 who is interactive and warm.

🔴 Critical Pre-Arrest Signs

CRITICAL
Narrowing pulse pressure (PP <25 mmHg) Early sign of low stroke volume/cardiac output. SBP falling while DBP rises = compensatory vasoconstriction. PP <25 = cardiogenic shock until proven otherwise.
CRITICAL
Sudden bradycardia after tachycardia Bezold-Jarisch reflex in hypovolemia. Pre-arrest vagal bradycardia. HR dropping below 60 in hypotensive patient = imminent arrest.
CRITICAL
ETCO₂ dropping <25 mmHg (intubated) Correlates with falling CO. ETCO₂ <10 = probable cardiac arrest. Trending down = diminishing perfusion before BP drops.
CRITICAL
SpO₂ labile / requires constant repositioning Poor peripheral perfusion → unreliable pulse ox. In shock, this means peripheral vasoconstriction is maximal.
CRITICAL
Sudden agitation → obtundation transition Agitation = brain trying to compensate. Obtundation = brain failing. This transition happens fast — often 5-10 minutes before arrest.
CRITICAL
Loss of radial pulse (SBP ~80) / femoral loss (SBP ~70) Classic teaching. Palpate both simultaneously. Absent radial + palpable femoral = SBP ~60-80 range.

🟡 Early Warning Signals

WARNING
Escalating vasopressor requirements NE >0.25 mcg/kg/min to maintain MAP 65 = refractory vasoplegic shock. Add vasopressin. If rising despite dual therapy = consider hydrocortisone, check adrenal insufficiency.
WARNING
Rising lactate despite treatment Lactate >4 = 40% hospital mortality. Failure to clear 10-20% per hour = inadequate resuscitation or ongoing tissue ischemia. Lactate rising despite vasopressors = dose escalation needed + look for source.
WARNING
New or worsening metabolic acidosis pH <7.2 with base deficit >-8 = severe tissue acidosis. Vasopressors become less effective as pH drops (<7.1 severely impairs α-receptor response).
WARNING
Oliguria worsening to anuria UO <0.3 ml/kg/hr for >2hrs despite adequate MAP = AKI-3 territory. Precedes multi-organ failure.
WARNING
Troponin rise in non-cardiac diagnosis Sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy. New wall motion abnormality on ECHO. LV dysfunction in distributive shock = switch phenotype, add dobutamine consideration.
WARNING
Temperature dysregulation (extreme fever or hypothermia) Temp >40.5°C or <36°C in sepsis = poor prognostic sign. Hypothermia in sepsis = very high mortality (often elderly, immunocompromised).

🩺 "Vasopressor Not Working" — Systematic Troubleshooting

Line Problems

  • Peripheral line infiltration? (look for local swelling/tissue necrosis)
  • Central line position confirmed? (CXR)
  • Air in line? Kinked tubing?
  • Wrong concentration mixed? Recalculate
  • Pump programming error? Verify rate + drug

Physiology Problems

  • Relative adrenal insufficiency (give hydrocortisone 200mg/day)
  • pH <7.1 → vasopressor resistance, correct acidosis
  • Severe hypocalcemia → give calcium chloride 1g IV
  • Tension pneumothorax? (needle decompress empirically if suspected)
  • Undrained tamponade or PE? Emergency intervention

Diagnosis Problems

  • Wrong shock type? Re-phenotype (new POCUS)
  • Occult hemorrhage? (eg. retroperitoneal)
  • Missed anaphylaxis component? Give epinephrine
  • Adrenal crisis? Thyroid storm? Addisonian crisis?
  • Drug interaction/OD causing vasodilation? (CCB, BB, etc)

Refractory Shock — Systematic Evaluation & Rescue

Structured approach to vasopressor failure: re-phenotyping, reversible cause identification, escalation strategies, and multi-organ dysfunction management.

🆘
Core principle: Refractory shock = the patient is not responding as expected. Stop, re-phenotype, identify the occult driver, then escalate systematically. Never escalate the same vasopressor blindly — incremental dose increases without re-evaluation are a diagnostic failure, not a treatment.

📉 Defining Refractory Shock — When Are You There?

Refractory Vasoplegic Shock

  • NE ≥0.25 mcg/kg/min AND MAP still <65 mmHg
  • Or NE rapidly escalating (doubling dose in <30 min)
  • Or already on 2 vasopressors at moderate doses
  • Action: Add vasopressin 0.03 u/min + hydrocortisone 200mg/day IV now

Refractory Cardiogenic Shock

  • NE + Dobu not achieving MAP ≥65 + CI <1.8 L/min/m²
  • PCWP >18 + SvO₂ <55% + rising lactate
  • Repeated POCUS: worsening LV/RV function
  • Action: Escalate to MCS (IABP, Impella, ECMO-team activation)

Multi-Vasopressor Shock

  • 3 vasopressors at high dose = ultra-refractory shock
  • NE + Vasopressin + Epi or AngII = exhausted receptor reserve
  • Mortality >70-80% in many series
  • Action: ECMO consult, re-goals-of-care conversation, methylene blue if vasoplegia

🔎 Systematic Re-Evaluation Protocol — "FAST-HUGS-CRASH"

When the patient is going south, run through this structured reassessment. Each step may reveal the hidden driver.

Hemodynamic Re-Phenotyping (POCUS)

  • Step 1 — LV function: Is the LV hyperdynamic (distributive) or hypokinetic (cardiogenic)? Has it changed since admission?
  • Step 2 — RV: New RV dilation? D-sign on short axis? Consider RV failure from ARDS, PE, or volume overload.
  • Step 3 — IVC: Plethoric (volume-overloaded) or flat (still hypovolemic)?
  • Step 4 — B-lines: New pulmonary edema on bilateral lung scan?
  • Step 5 — Valves: New severe MR/TR? Aortic dissection? Tamponade?

Reversible Causes Checklist (HITS)

  • H — Hemorrhage: Occult? GI, retroperitoneal, surgical site, hemothorax? Check Hb trend.
  • H — Hypovolemia: Inadequate resuscitation vs over-resuscitation (abdominal compartment syndrome)?
  • I — Ischemia: Mesenteric ischemia (rising lactate + abdominal pain)? Limb ischemia from vasopressors?
  • I — Infection spread: Source not controlled? New infection? Antibiotic coverage gap?
  • T — Tension PTX / Tamponade: Re-examine. Repeat POCUS. Needle if suspected.
  • T — Toxin/Drug: Accidental CCB/BB overdose? New drug interaction? Adrenal suppressant (etomidate, ketoconazole)?
  • S — Sodium / electrolytes: Severe hypocalcemia, hypophosphatemia (reduces myocardial contractility)?
  • S — Steroid insufficiency: Relative adrenal insufficiency — give HC 200mg/day empirically if refractory.

Hemodynamic Monitoring Escalation

  • Arterial line: Mandatory in all patients on ≥ moderate vasopressors (beat-to-beat MAP). Also enables ABG/labs without repeated sticks.
  • Central venous catheter: For multi-vasopressor therapy, CVP monitoring, ScvO₂, medication management.
  • Pulse contour CO (PiCCO, LiDCO, FloTrac): When CO estimate needed without PAC. Requires arterial line.
  • Pulmonary artery catheter (PAC): Controversial but useful in complex mixed shock (cardiogenic + distributive), ARDS + high PEEP, refractory shock phenotyping. Gives CO, PCWP, PVR, SvO₂.
  • Serial POCUS: Most accessible real-time monitor. Repeat q2-4h in deteriorating patients.

🪜 Vasopressor Escalation Ladder — Step by Step

⚠️
Each step should include: reassessment of diagnosis, fluid status check, and ruling out reversible causes. Don't skip straight to the top.
Step 1 — First Response
Norepinephrine 0.01–0.1 mcg/kg/min + 500ml balanced crystalloid bolus if fluid-responsive
Titrate NE every 3-5 min. Check POCUS IVC. Reassess lactate and MAP in 15 min. Peripheral IV acceptable here.
Step 2 — NE 0.15–0.25 mcg/kg/min
Add Vasopressin 0.03 u/min (fixed). Get central line if not already done.
Vasopressin allows NE dose reduction ~35%. Start hydrocortisone 200mg/day IV (50mg q6h or infusion) — SSC 2026 supports early steroid at NE ≥0.25.
Step 3 — NE >0.25 + Vasopressin
Re-phenotype aggressively. Consider Epinephrine 0.05–0.2 mcg/kg/min (especially if CO low). Fludrocortisone 50mcg daily if adrenal crisis suspected.
POCUS reassessment mandatory here. LV dysfunction? Add dobutamine. Arrhythmia driving shock? Cardiovert. Lactate still rising? Source control emergency.
Step 4 — Triple Vasopressor Refractory
Angiotensin II 2.5–80 ng/kg/min (catecholamine-independent pathway). Methylene blue 1.5–2 mg/kg IV bolus for vasoplegia.
ATHOS-3: AngII reduces catecholamine burden. Methylene blue: inhibits NO/iNOS pathway in vasoplegia — particularly useful post-cardiopulmonary bypass or anaphylaxis-refractory states.
Step 5 — Ultra-Refractory / ECMO Candidacy
VA-ECMO consideration for refractory cardiogenic shock. VV-ECMO for severe ARDS + hemodynamic compromise. MCS consult.
ECMO provides circulatory support while treating underlying cause. Eligibility: age, comorbidities, reversibility of underlying disease, cannulation site. Goals-of-care conversation must happen at this point.

🫁 Multi-Organ Failure — Vasopressor Adjustments per Organ

Cardiac — New LV Dysfunction

  • Presentation: POCUS EF <40% new, rising troponin, ScvO₂ <60%, low CO despite MAP maintained
  • Cause: Sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy (reversible in 7-14 days), demand ischemia from vasopressors, ACS
  • Adjustment: Ensure MAP ≥65 first, then add dobutamine 2.5-5 mcg/kg/min. If NE causing excessive HR ↑ demand ischemia → consider epi instead (more balanced α+β). ECG for ACS. Troponin-positive? Cardiology consult.
  • Target: CI >2.2, ScvO₂ >65%, lactate clearing

Renal — AKI / Oliguria

  • First: Ensure MAP ≥65 (consider 70+ if baseline HTN). MAP is the primary renal perfusion driver.
  • AVOID "renal dose" dopamine — obsolete, no benefit proven
  • Vasopressin benefit: May constrict efferent arteriole → maintain GFR at low MAP. Add if NE already ≥0.25.
  • AKI Stage 3: Early RRT consult. Fluid overload threshold for dialysis: >10% cumulative fluid balance → worse outcomes
  • Watch: Drug dosing adjustment (milrinone, renally cleared — reduce dose with AKI)

RV Failure — New or Worsening

  • Triggers: ARDS (high PEEP), PE, volume overload, sepsis-induced RV depression
  • POCUS signs: RV:LV ratio >0.6, D-sign (interventricular septal shift), IVC plethoric
  • Vasopressor choice: Vasopressin preferred (↓PVR relative to NE). Avoid epinephrine alone (↑PVR).
  • Inotrope: Milrinone preferred (↓PVR + ↑RV contractility). Low-dose dobutamine as alternative.
  • Avoid: Excess fluid (worsens RV dilation). Minimize PEEP. Prone positioning if ARDS-driven.

Liver / Mesenteric Ischemia

  • Risk: High-dose NE + vasopressin → splanchnic vasoconstriction → hepatic/bowel ischemia
  • Signs: Rising LFTs, direct hyperbilirubinemia, new abdominal pain/distension, rising lactate without other cause, bloody stool
  • Management: Reduce vasopressin first (most implicated). Lower NE if MAP allows. Urgent surgical/GI consult if mesenteric ischemia suspected.
  • Hepatic failure: Consider NAC infusion (antioxidant), avoid hepatotoxic drugs, early nutrition support (enteral if tolerated)

ARDS — Ventilation + Vasopressor Interaction

  • High PEEP ↓ venous return: May need higher vasopressor dose — expected, not a sign of worsening shock alone
  • Prone positioning: Can improve oxygenation AND reduce vasopressor requirements by reducing RV afterload and improving V/Q
  • Inhaled vasodilators (iNO, prostacyclin): For RV failure + refractory hypoxia. Reduces PVR selectively without systemic hypotension.
  • Avoid: Excessive tidal volumes (barotrauma worsens RV strain). Target Vt 6ml/kg IBW, Pplat ≤30 cmH₂O.

Peripheral Limb Ischemia from Vasopressors

  • Signs: Mottled/cyanotic fingers/toes, cold extremity distal to vasopressor infusion, absent Doppler signals
  • Prevention: Use lowest effective dose. Wean when MAP allows. Rotate vasopressors (vasopressin + NE instead of high-dose NE alone).
  • Management: Reduce vasopressor dose if MAP allows. Phentolamine (α-blocker) 5-10mg SC/intraarterial at affected site. Nitroglycerin paste 2% topically to affected extremity. Heparin infusion if documented thrombosis. Vascular surgery consult early.
  • Fingers: Document baseline hourly. Severe digital ischemia = reduce vasopressin first.

🔵 Methylene Blue — Rescue Vasoplegia Agent

Mechanism & Indications

  • Inhibits NO synthase (iNOS) and guanylyl cyclase → reduces pathological vasodilation
  • Indications: Refractory vasoplegia on ≥3 vasopressors, post-cardiopulmonary bypass vasoplegia, protamine/drug-induced vasoplegia, anaphylaxis refractory to epinephrine
  • Not a first-line agent — rescue only

Dosing & Administration

  • Bolus: 1.5–2 mg/kg IV over 20-60 min
  • Infusion (if needed): 0.25–2 mg/kg/hr
  • Response: MAP response typically within 30-60 min
  • Contraindications: G6PD deficiency (hemolysis), severe renal failure (accumulation), concurrent serotonergic drugs (serotonin syndrome)
  • Side effects: Urine/secretions turn blue-green (warn staff/family), SpO₂ falsely LOW (stains pulse ox — use ABG), nausea, pain at infusion site

📉 De-escalation — When & How to Safely Wean

✅
Premature de-escalation causes rebound shock. Too slow de-escalation = excess vasopressor exposure, ischemia, and ICU complications. Structured weaning is essential.

Criteria to Begin Weaning

  • MAP ≥65 mmHg consistently for ≥2–4 hrs
  • Lactate clearing (<2 mmol/L or ≥10% clearance/hr)
  • Source of shock treated (antibiotics + source control)
  • Urine output ≥0.5 ml/kg/hr
  • Improving mentation and perfusion exam
  • POCUS: stable LV function, IVC not collapsing with each breath

Weaning Order (Reverse of Addition)

  • 1st to wean: Most recently added agent
  • Steroids: Wean hydrocortisone after vasopressor-free for 24h (or taper per protocol)
  • Vasopressin: Wean before NE (come off 0.03 → 0.02 → off over 2-4h)
  • NE last: Reduce by 0.02-0.05 mcg/kg/min every 15-30 min. Watch for rebound.
  • Inotropes: Wean dobutamine slowly — abrupt stop can cause rebound cardiogenic deterioration

Oral Vasopressor Transition

  • Midodrine (α1 agonist, oral): Can facilitate IV vasopressor weaning in patients stabilizing. Dose: 5-15mg TID PO. SSC 2026: insufficient evidence for routine use, but emerging practice.
  • Start midodrine when NE <0.05 mcg/kg/min
  • Allow 1-2h overlap before removing IV vasopressor
  • Monitor for urinary retention (α1 bladder effect)
  • Only in patients who can take PO or via NG tube

🤝 Goals of Care — The Conversation You Must Have

💬
SSC 2026 explicitly includes goals-of-care conversations as part of comprehensive sepsis management. When vasopressor doses are escalating without response, this conversation is a clinical obligation, not an optional add-on.

Triggers for GOC Discussion

  • 3 or more vasopressors at high dose
  • Escalating vasopressor requirements despite all reversible causes addressed
  • New or progressive multi-organ failure (≥3 organs)
  • Patient not meeting ECMO/MCS candidacy criteria
  • Underlying condition not reversible (metastatic cancer, severe frailty, devastating brain injury)
  • Family/patient known advance directives

Key Principles

  • Frame as "what the patient would want" not "what we should do"
  • Honest prognosis: "Despite everything we are doing, he/she is not improving. We believe..."
  • Time-limited trial: "Let's give the next 6 hours and reassess together"
  • Comfort-focused care does not mean abandonment — reframe as active comfort
  • If de-escalating: structured vasopressor weaning with comfort medications (e.g., morphine for air hunger)
  • Document discussion, family understanding, and agreed plan in detail

Sepsis Protocol — SSC 2026

Updated 129-statement guideline published March 2026. Key changes from 2021, vasopressor sequencing, new antibiotic timing framework, and monitoring targets.

📌
Sepsis-3 Definition (unchanged): Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysregulated host response to infection. SOFA score ↑ ≥2 from baseline. Septic shock = sepsis + vasopressor requirement to maintain MAP ≥65 + lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate volume resuscitation. NEW 2026: Standardized certainty language — "definite / probable / possible / unlikely sepsis" used throughout.
🆕
What Changed from SSC 2021 → 2026: 129 total statements (46 new). 3 statements differ from prior guidance. Key shifts: (1) antibiotic timing now risk-stratified by certainty of diagnosis; (2) vasopressors may be started concurrently with fluids in unstable shock; (3) MAP target 60-65 for patients ≥65 yrs old; (4) no longer routine albumin with crystalloids; (5) stronger emphasis on POCUS and dynamic fluid assessment; (6) prehospital antibiotics in high-risk patients with long transfer times; (7) NEWS/NEWS2/MEWS preferred over qSOFA for screening.

🎯 SSC 2026 Monitoring Targets

  • MAP ≥65 mmHg (STRONG). NEW 2026: age ≥65 → MAP target 60-65 mmHg acceptable (CONDITIONAL).
  • Invasive BP monitoring (arterial line) suggested when: intermediate-to-high vasopressor dose, escalating/multiple vasopressors, frequent ABG, inconsistent noninvasive readings. Otherwise, noninvasive acceptable.
  • Lactate clearance (CONDITIONAL) — serial lactate to guide resuscitation. No specific clearance rate mandated.
  • CRT normalization — CRT as adjunct perfusion marker alongside lactate
  • Dynamic fluid measures preferred over static CVP or exam alone
  • Source control within 6 hrs if anatomical source identified
  • Glucose 140-180 mg/dL — insulin infusion if >180 × 2 readings
  • SOFA reassessment q24h — no improvement = review coverage, source
  • De-escalate antibiotics (STRONG) when microbiological diagnosis + sensitivities available. Suggest de-escalation even when cultures negative (CONDITIONAL).
  • Procalcitonin + clinical evaluation (CONDITIONAL) to decide WHEN to STOP antibiotics (not to start them). Shorter courses preferred if source controlled.

🆕 New SSC 2026 Statements (Highlights)

  • Screening: Use NEWS, NEWS2, MEWS, or SIRS over qSOFA as single screening tool (STRONG — changed from 2021)
  • Fluid type: Crystalloids alone over crystalloids + supplemental albumin for resuscitation (CONDITIONAL — revised from 2021 which suggested albumin with large volumes)
  • Antibiotics — no anaerobic coverage needed if no anaerobic infection risk factors (CONDITIONAL — new nuance)
  • Beta-lactam prolonged infusion for maintenance after loading dose (STRONG — new 2026)
  • Awake proning for sepsis-induced hypoxic respiratory failure (new statement)
  • Midodrine for vasopressor weaning: insufficient evidence for routine use (new statement)
  • Post-discharge: Written + verbal education for sepsis survivors before discharge (CONDITIONAL — new post-sepsis domain)
  • VTE prophylaxis: LMWH preferred over UFH if no contraindication
  • Vitamin C IV: Suggest AGAINST routine use (CONDITIONAL — changed from 2021)

⚠️ Sepsis SSC 2026 — Key Pitfalls Updated

🕳️ PIT — Treating "possible" sepsis like confirmed septic shock
2026 now distinguishes: "possible sepsis without shock" gets a 1-3h assessment window before antibiotics if infection likelihood is low. Don't reflexively give broad-spectrum abx to every febrile patient — stewardship matters. Use clinical judgment + certainty framework.
🕳️ PIT — Routing albumin with all resuscitation
SSC 2026 changed: now suggests crystalloids ALONE over crystalloids + albumin. Reserve albumin for patients who already received large crystalloid volumes or cirrhosis patients. This was updated from 2021 which suggested albumin when large volumes given.
🕳️ PIT — Using IV Vitamin C routinely
SSC 2026 now suggests AGAINST routine IV Vitamin C in septic shock (CITRIS-ALI, VITAMINS trials showed no benefit). This is a reversal from the earlier enthusiasm. Don't include it in your bundle unless specific circumstances.
💎 PEARL — Concurrent vasopressor + fluids in unstable shock
SSC 2026 explicitly states: in "unstable septic shock" (mottling, altered mentation, cyanosis, severely low BP) — start vasopressor CONCURRENTLY with fluid, not sequentially. Don't finish your fluid bolus before starting NE in the crashing patient.
💎 PEARL — Prolonged beta-lactam infusion (STRONG 2026)
Extended infusion of piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem, cefepime over 3-4 hours (after loading bolus) achieves better PK/PD targets. This is now a STRONG recommendation in SSC 2026 — implement in your ICU/ER pharmacy protocols.
💎 PEARL — Age ≥65: lower MAP target acceptable
NEW 2026: For patients ≥65 years, MAP target 60-65 mmHg is acceptable and may reduce vasopressor exposure in elderly patients. This acknowledges physiological differences in older patients and avoids unnecessary catecholamine burden.

Mixing, Preparation & Administration

Standard concentrations, dilution protocols, compatibility, syringe pump setup, and central vs peripheral access requirements.

⚗️
General Rule: All vasopressors should ideally run through a central venous catheter (CVC) for continuous infusion. Peripheral administration is acceptable as a bridge (≤24h) via large-bore proximal vein. NEVER run undiluted concentrated vasopressor through peripheral IV without dilution.

🔌 Compatibility & Administration Rules

Compatible Flush Solutions

  • Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl): Compatible with all vasopressors
  • Dextrose 5% (D5W): Compatible with most (epinephrine, dopamine, dobutamine preferred)
  • Lactated Ringer's: Avoid with vasopressin (precipitate risk)
  • Use NS as the default flush solution between drug changes

Y-Site Compatibility (Common Combos)

  • NE + Vasopressin: ✅ Compatible Y-site
  • NE + Dobutamine: ✅ Compatible Y-site
  • NE + Midazolam/Fentanyl: ✅ Compatible
  • Epi + Bicarbonate: ❌ Incompatible (inactivates)
  • Dopamine + Sodium Bicarbonate: ❌ Incompatible
  • Phenylephrine + Alkaline solutions: ❌ Avoid

Syringe Pump Setup Rules

  • Use dedicated lumen for vasopressors — never run through same lumen as intermittent drugs
  • Label syringe with: drug name, concentration (mcg/ml), date/time prepared
  • Standard syringe: 50ml BD or equivalent with lock
  • Change syringe/tubing q24h (or per local protocol)
  • Never flush vasopressor tubing — it delivers a bolus. Change the line.
  • Double-nurse check before initiating and all dose changes (per most protocols)

Epinephrine Formulations — Critical Distinction

  • 1:1000 = 1mg/ml → IM injection only for anaphylaxis (0.3-0.5mg IM mid-lateral thigh)
  • 1:10,000 = 0.1mg/ml → IV bolus for cardiac arrest (1mg = 10ml of this)
  • Infusion: Dilute to standard concentration (see drug detail) → syringe pump
  • NEVER give 1:1000 (1mg/ml) undiluted IV — fatal hypertensive crisis risk
  • For anaphylaxis with IV access: use 1:10,000 (0.1mg/ml) IV very slowly or dilute 1:1000 to get 1:10,000 first

📋 Titration Principles

Upward Titration

  • Titrate in small increments: 0.02–0.05 mcg/kg/min for NE
  • Allow 3–5 minutes for haemodynamic effect before next adjustment
  • Avoid single large bolus-like increments — rebound hypertension risk
  • Re-assess volume status before each dose increase
  • Document: time, dose change, clinical indication, BP/MAP response
  • For Angiotensin II: uptitrate by 2.5–5 ng/kg/min every 5 min
  • For Milrinone: no loading in shock — start infusion directly at 0.125–0.25 mcg/kg/min

De-escalation Strategy

  • Begin weaning when MAP ≥65 consistently ≥2hrs + improving lactate
  • Decrease by 25–50% increments, never abrupt discontinuation
  • Wean the most recently added agent first (last-in, first-out)
  • Wean vasopressin before norepinephrine
  • Allow 15–30 min between decrements on the final agent
  • Milrinone: allow 2–3h between dose reductions (long half-life)

Mandatory Cessation Criteria

  • MAP persistently >110–120 mmHg — reduce urgently
  • New ischaemia: ECG changes, limb ischaemia, digital cyanosis
  • Developing digital/limb necrosis — consider phentolamine (5–10mg SC at site)
  • Drug-induced refractory arrhythmia (esp. epinephrine, dopamine)
  • Patient goals-of-care transition to comfort measures
  • Milrinone: persistent hypotension despite vasopressor titration

Clinical Pearls & Pitfalls

High-yield evidence-based wisdom and avoidable errors in vasopressor management — condensed from trials, meta-analyses, and bedside experience.

💎 Clinical Pearls

💎 NE via peripheral IV is safe short-term
Multiple RCTs and meta-analyses confirm NE via antecubital or distal large vein is safe for ≤24h. Diluted to standard concentration. Don't delay for CVC. Monitor for extravasation q1h. Phentolamine 5-10mg subcutaneous if extravasation occurs.
💎 Vasopressin spares NE dose
Adding vasopressin 0.03-0.04 u/min allows NE dose reduction ~35%. Important to reduce NE-associated arrhythmias, tachycardia, and ischemia. VASST trial: benefit greatest in less severe shock (NE 0.14–0.31). Add earlier than you think.
💎 Epi for anaphylaxis — IM is faster than IV
IM epinephrine (0.5mg 1:1000 mid-lateral thigh) achieves peak plasma levels faster than IV in most scenarios and is the recommended route. Only use IV in cardiac arrest/imminent arrest or when IV is already established and IM is impractical.
💎 Norepinephrine over dopamine in septic shock
SOAP II (De Backer 2010, NEJM): NE superior to dopamine — less arrhythmias, trend to improved survival. Dopamine reserved for patients with absolute or relative bradycardia (HR <80 in shock) or cardiogenic shock with bradycardia.
💎 Phenylephrine in HOCM/dynamic LVOTO
Phenylephrine (pure alpha) is PREFERRED in hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy and dynamic LVOT obstruction. Increases SVR without inotropic effect, reduces outflow tract gradient. NE and especially Epi worsen obstruction due to inotropic effects.
💎 Rule of 6 (old ICU trick, modified)
Intuitive dosing aid: 6mg drug in 100ml = 1mcg/ml. Running at 1ml/hr = 1mcg/min. Easy bedside mental math check. Verify against your institutional standard.
💎 Calcium in vasoplegia / CCB toxicity
Calcium chloride 1g IV (or calcium gluconate 3g) is a vasopressor-like adjunct in vasodilatory shock, beta-blocker/CCB toxicity, and hypocalcemia. Often overlooked. Gives a transient hemodynamic boost while other agents are being titrated.
💎 Milrinone over Dobutamine in beta-blocker users and RHF
Milrinone bypasses β-receptors entirely (PDE-3 mechanism) — it remains effective when dobutamine fails due to receptor downregulation in chronic HF or in patients on beta-blockers. Also reduces PVR significantly, making it preferred in RV failure with elevated pulmonary pressures. Its Achilles heel: long half-life makes it hard to reverse in haemodynamically unstable patients.
💎 Angiotensin II — the catecholamine-independent pathway
ATHOS-3 trial: AngII significantly improved MAP and reduced catecholamine burden in vasodilatory shock patients. Particularly useful in: (1) catecholamine-refractory vasoplegia, (2) patients with high renin states (post-ACEI use, septic shock), (3) when receptor exhaustion limits NE response. Remember: nanogram dosing — errors from mcg/ng confusion are dangerous.
💎 Vasopressin de-escalation before norepinephrine
When weaning multi-vasopressor therapy, always stop vasopressin before reducing norepinephrine. Vasopressin lacks the titratability of NE and has organ ischaemia risk with prolonged use. Wean vasopressin to off first, then slowly reduce NE last.

🕳️ Clinical Pits (Dangerous Errors)

🕳️ PIT — 1:1000 epinephrine IV without dilution
This is potentially fatal. 1mg/ml IV causes severe hypertension, CVA, arrhythmia, myocardial ischemia. Always dilute to 1:10,000 (0.1mg/ml) for IV use. Verify formulation before every administration. In anaphylaxis: IM 1:1000 FIRST unless cardiac arrest.
🕳️ PIT — Not correcting the cause
Vasopressors treat the MAP, not the disease. Escalating NE in a patient with undrained empyema, ongoing hemorrhage, or tension pneumothorax will kill them. Source control is the most important intervention. Vasopressors buy time.
🕳️ PIT — Flushing vasopressor lines
Flushing NE or epi-containing tubing delivers a concentrated bolus. This causes dangerous hemodynamic spikes. If you need to change the syringe, pause the pump and change the entire setup, prime new tubing carefully.
🕳️ PIT — Dopamine in ICU patients for renal protection
"Renal dose" dopamine (1-3 mcg/kg/min) has NO evidence to improve renal outcomes and increases risk of arrhythmias and tachycardia. This practice is obsolete. Use NE ± vasopressin and maintain adequate MAP for kidney perfusion.
🕳️ PIT — Vasopressin as first-line or titrated high-dose
Vasopressin should be fixed-dose (0.03–0.04 u/min). High doses cause splanchnic and coronary ischemia, skin necrosis, and hyponatremia. Don't titrate it up. Don't use as first-line alone in septic shock.
🕳️ PIT — Giving dobutamine alone in septic shock without vasopressor
Dobutamine is a vasodilator + inotrope. In vasodilatory septic shock, adding dobutamine without adequate vasopressor support will drop BP further. Ensure MAP is ≥65 with NE before adding dobutamine for low CO states.
🕳️ PIT — Ignoring limb ischemia
Digital and limb ischemia from vasopressor-induced vasoconstriction occurs — check extremities q2-4h. High NE + vasopressin combination is especially implicated. Consider dose reduction, nitroglycerin patch to affected area, or phentolamine. Document findings.
🕳️ PIT — Milrinone loading dose in haemodynamically unstable patients
The standard milrinone loading dose (50 mcg/kg over 10–20 min) causes profound hypotension in hypotensive patients. In shock or borderline BP, skip the loading dose entirely and start the maintenance infusion directly at 0.125–0.25 mcg/kg/min. The long half-life (2.5h) means effects build gradually without requiring a bolus.
🕳️ PIT — Angiotensin II mcg vs ng dosing confusion
Angiotensin II (Giapreza) is dosed in nanograms per kg per minute — 1000× less concentrated than mcg dosing used for other vasopressors. A dose of 20 ng/kg/min is normal; entering this as 20 mcg/kg/min on a pump would deliver a 1000-fold overdose. Always double-check the unit when programming. Label syringes clearly with "ng/kg/min".

🎛️ Receptor → Hemodynamic Effect

α1 Receptor (Postsynaptic)

  • Location: Vascular smooth muscle (arteries & veins)
  • Effect: Vasoconstriction → ↑SVR → ↑MAP
  • Also: Skin, GI, renal vasoconstriction (risk of ischemia)
  • Drugs: NE, Epi (high dose), Phen (pure), Dopa (high dose)

β1 Receptor (Cardiac)

  • Location: SA node, AV node, ventricular myocardium
  • Effect: ↑HR (chronotropy), ↑contractility (inotropy), ↑conduction
  • Risk: Tachyarrhythmia, increased myocardial O₂ demand
  • Drugs: Epi, Dobutamine, Dopamine (moderate dose), NE (mild)

β2 Receptor (Vascular/Pulmonary)

  • Location: Vascular smooth muscle (skeletal, hepatic, pulmonary)
  • Effect: Vasodilation → ↓SVR; bronchodilation
  • Epi low dose: β2 dominant → vasodilation, ↑CO, ↓DBP
  • Epi high dose: α1 dominant → vasoconstriction
